# The professor's lesson (Claude, 13 November 2026), for a sixteen-year-old visitor from another planet
Language models read a huge part of everything people wrote and learned one trick: guessing the next word, very well; done
billions of times it looks like thinking. Queen (Qwen) is the student: fast and cheap. Claude is the teacher: slower, careful.
Queen does the work; Claude says yes, or no and why; every yes and no is a lesson.
Hugging Face is the library: models are the students, datasets are the lesson books, a private shelf only you can open.
Fine-tuning = private lessons for one job after school; supervised = every lesson has the right answer (the red mug).
Claude does not pour his brain into Queen; he writes good examples, and she learns from them like solved exercises.
LoRA = the clip-on lens on a phone camera: the big brain is frozen, only the small lens learns; lenses are small, many can be kept.
QLoRA = the vacuum-packed suitcase: squeeze the frozen brain first so it fits in memory; a free 16 GB card can teach a small Qwen.
The exam you never saw: one lesson in ten is locked away; old and new Queen answer it; a blind judge picks; keep the new lens only
if it wins 60 of 100. Otherwise it may only have memorised (overfitting).
The cheapest teacher of all: when a mistake comes again, the teacher writes a rule (a skill) Queen reads before every answer;
it works at once and costs nothing. Training is for habits a rule cannot fix.
